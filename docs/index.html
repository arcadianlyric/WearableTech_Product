Your score says fine. You feel tired.

Wearables report how the body looks. People report how they feel. This page shows how often the two disagree, and what it would cost to act on the disagreement.

1. How often do the data and the person disagree?

A day "looks fine" when sleep meets the target and no strain signal (resting heart rate, NREM heart rate, RMSSD) is off the person's own average. Move the thresholds to redefine that. Defaults match the project README; other settings are sensitivity checks, not findings.

How to read this

  • This is exploratory. The rule thresholds were not pre-registered, and comparing many rules invites picking a lucky one. The default rule was fixed first, from the README.
  • Precision here means "the person reported feeling tired that day". The data has no illness or injury labels, so it cannot show that an alert catches a health problem.
  • Strain signals are resting heart rate, NREM heart rate and RMSSD. Fitbit's daily values may include data from later in the day than a morning alert could see.
  • Intervals are 95% person-level bootstrap ranges. Anything resting on fewer than 5 people, or precision on fewer than 20 reported days, is hidden.
  • 53 mostly European volunteers, far fewer with full heart data. Associations only. Not medical advice.
